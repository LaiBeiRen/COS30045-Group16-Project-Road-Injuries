# Proposed usability test protocol

Status: planned participant evaluation. Browser functionality checks do not replace this evaluation.

Purpose: determine whether a reader can navigate the story, interpret the measures, use relevant controls and recognise limits on comparisons.

Recruit a small set of readers representing your target audience. Record the actual participant count, recruitment, consent process and device used. Ask participants to think aloud; avoid coaching during tasks.

## Tasks

1. Find the road-user chart. Inspect 2020 and identify the hospitalisation count for light vehicle occupants. Expected: 17,592 in the supplied file.
2. Compare growth for vulnerable road users and light vehicle occupants since 2011. Explain what an index of 100 means. Expected: 23.4% versus 11.5% endpoint increases; the baseline is 2011.
3. Show female records in the age chart, then sort by volume. Identify the largest group. Expected: ages 40–64, 44,796 hospitalisations across the combined period.
4. In the remoteness chart, select 2021. Compare counts and bed days per hospitalisation. Expected: Major Cities has the largest count; Remote has the highest supplied ratio, approximately 5.89 days.
5. Explain whether the remoteness chart shows crash location and whether counts prove individual risk. Expected: usual residence; population/exposure denominators are absent.
6. Find a reporting-comparability note and download a chart selection.
